How PORE works

Font
Your data

Refining combines two copies of the same item. The base item goes up one level and gains stats from the material item. A "perfect" refine uses two identical items, so a perfect P10 normally eats 512 base items (1 → 2 → 4 → … → 512).

World boss weapons can only be refined up to P5 (16 base items the standard way), so PORE lists them in their own group and caps their plans at P5. Their refining fees are paid in world boss tokens (WB) instead of gold: 125 at P2, 250 at P3, 375 at P4 and 500 at P5.

PORE searches every other way to combine items. Because stat gains are rounded down and every refine adds at least +2, weaker materials often reach the very same stats. The planner keeps only the cheapest way to reach each stat combination at each level and builds up to your target, so the answer is the cheapest plan under the chosen formula.

Stat formula (per stat)

new = floor( base + max(2, material × rate) )

The result is one level above the base. The material can be any level up to the base level. Each stat uses the rate of its category:

Stats not listed on the wiki's refining page (elemental damage, mana regeneration, node or monster encounter chance, critical evade) are assigned a category by best guess. They are marked with a dashed outline on the item card and you can change the category there.

±1 surprises and the formula lab

The game sometimes lands one point off this formula, most likely because it evaluates the same maths in floating point, or applies one penalty ratio to every stat of a multi-stat item. PORE handles this three ways:

  • Record what you got. Tap the pencil on any step and enter the real in-game result. PORE re-plans everything downstream with your number.
  • Safe planning. The "Safe" switch in the Target card plans with the lowest result of every plausible variant, so no refine can come up short at a rounding boundary.
  • Formula lab. Every recorded observation is tested against all candidate formulas. When one variant explains your observations better than the default, the lab shows it and you can switch to it.

What the plan counts

  • Base items (P1): unrefined copies you must collect. This is what PORE minimises.
  • Gold: the sum of refining fees from the wiki table (250 gold at level 2 up to 3,000 gold at level 10).
  • Charm factor: how much the base success chance must be multiplied to reach 100%. Charms stack multiplicatively, so a ×20 could be a ×4 Clover with a ×5 Horseshoe. Multi-refining divides the chance by the number of items in the batch (success = base × charm ÷ items), so a batch of N needs N times the factor, but only one set of charms per action. You can stack any number of charms (up to 5 types per refine) and they are used up either way, so PORE refines each batch in one action with the fewest charms that reach 100%, using only the charms you switch on.

Items you already have

Owned items are used for free wherever they fit, and their quantity is respected exactly for small inventories. With very large inventories PORE caps the search and slots the leftovers in greedily. The plan is then marked "approximate": still valid, possibly a little more expensive than the true optimum.

Labels

P4 means a level-4 item with perfect stats. L4 means a level-4 item whose stats are below perfect. Every chip shows the exact stats the item must have, so you can check your inventory against it. ⚠ test this marks a refine that sits on a rounding boundary: the plausible formula variants disagree there, so the game could give ±1. Test those first and record the result with the pencil.
